## Voiceover (paste into TTS)
<!-- VO:START -->
[low, tense, almost a whisper] You just fell off a cruise ship. It's 2:14 a.m. Nobody saw.

[flat, cold, letting it land] And the ship didn't stop.

[quick, factual, building urgency] Deck five is fifty feet above the water. You hit it at almost forty miles an hour.

[breathless, panicked] You come up choking on salt water. Every part of you says the same thing: swim after it.

[calm, confident, slowing down] Here's what you don't know. That instinct kills people. Panic swimming burns you out in minutes. Rule one: lie back, spread your arms, and float.

[quiet, steadier] So you float.

[desperate, then deflated] You scream for help. The engines are louder.

[brisk, matter-of-fact] The ship is doing twenty-three miles an hour. Every minute, it's another third of a mile away. About twenty people go overboard from cruise ships every year. Many aren't missed until breakfast.

[dry, ominous] Breakfast is five hours away.

[curious, pulling the listener in] So why were you on deck five at 2 a.m.? Because your brother Marcus texted you.

[reading a text, casual] "Back of the ship. Deck five. Two a.m. I need to tell you something."

[suspicious, wry] Marcus paid for this whole trip. Marcus has never paid for anything.

[slow, lonely] He never showed up. So you leaned on the rail and waited.

[sharp, then slowing to a hush] You didn't slip. Two hands. Flat on your back. One hard shove. And right before they hit you, a man's voice whispered,

[whispering, cold, close to the ear] "Bye, Marcus."

[very slow, quiet, chilling] You're not Marcus.

[direct, urgent but clear] The ship's lights are still in sight. Swim for them, and burn everything you've got. Or float, save your strength, and watch them disappear.

[lighter, inviting] Comment SWIM or FLOAT. The majority writes Part 2.

[low, teasing, mysterious] And that yellow jacket Marcus made you wear tonight? Something is zipped inside it.
<!-- VO:END -->
